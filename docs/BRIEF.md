# Challenge brief: Team coordination platform

Read this once, in ten minutes. Ask anything in the WhatsApp group. Then build.

## Who we are

We are 20 volunteers running an institute. Each member belongs to one of three tiers, Shapers GO!, Shapers Impact and Shapers Core, with its own weekly commitment of hours. To run the institute we hold six recurring meeting series: Summer School, Grants, Shape Talks, Community, Shapers Tech and Shapers Voice. We talk on Slack and schedule everything in Google Calendar.

## What hurts

Our tasks live on a Miro board. A task cannot be assigned to a person, nobody can see their own tasks in one place, and when a card moves, its history is gone. We work daily in Impact Hub, work hours are not tracked at all, and with 20 people nobody can tell who is active and who has drifted away. Meeting attendance is kept by hand in a spreadsheet, and keeping it up costs one person's time every week. Every meeting is announced by hand, and nobody gets a reminder.

## What we want

One platform we will use from the week after the hackathon.

## What we seek

### 1. Everyone knows what they are working on

As a member, I open the platform and see tasks along projects and in one place every task that is mine, across all projects. I can finish it, change its priority, date or hand it to someone.

As an admin, I see all projects and their tasks on one board, add a project in one step, assign a task to a person, and see how many tasks each member finished and when.

### 2. We know who is putting in the hours

As a member, I log the hours in a few seconds and see where I stand against my tier's weekly commitment.

As an admin, I see who is behind this week, and any member's history.

### 3. We know who showed up

As a member, I can clock in at the start of every meeting in the matter of seconds. I can also see which meetings I attended, joined remotely, or missed, excused or not.

As an admin, I also record attendance for any meeting with as little effort as possible, and I see everyone's record across all six meeting series.

### Across all three

As an admin, I look at hours, attendance and tasks for every member, across tiers and meeting series and over time, and see how the organisation is doing. Make analytics useful from the perspective of the leader.

## Constraints

- Meetings stay in Google Calendar. Read them from there. Do not create or edit them.

- Communication stays in Slack. Read from it if you need to. The core platform writes nothing to Slack. Posting to Slack is an extra, below.

- AI integration is welcome where it makes a task easier, and unwelcome where it does not.

## Extras

Only once all areas work, these earn extra points:

Focus on the gamification aspect of the app. Member hours and attendance: streaks, badges, anything playful.

Bonus features:

- Meeting announcements and reminders in Slack, 3 days and 1 day before.

- A Slack activity signal in the admin view: does a member react, post, and show up as active. Admin only, never shown to members.

- Jarvis, personal agent. Think about where agents could be implemented in this whole platform. Talk with Nik about creative ideas.

You can also add features that improve the workflow of the team.

Extras raise usefulness and quality. They never replace a missing area.

## What you get

- The sandbox package, sent by email: a setup page, a calendar file with all six meeting series, a roster of 20 members with their tier and weekly commitment, six weeks of attendance in the structure we use today, six weeks of made-up hours, and ten project names. No real people, no real data. Import the calendar file into your own Google account. For the Slack extras, make your own free workspace; the setup page says how.

- A WhatsApp group where we answer questions. Everyone sees every answer.

- Your own stack, hosting and accounts. We provide no credits.

## What you hand in, Sunday 14:00

- The running platform at a public URL, connected to your calendar and Slack workspace where it needs them.

- A public repository with all the code.

- One page describing the infrastructure: services, how they connect, the names of environment variables, how to deploy from zero.

- A 10-minute live demo on the sandbox data, showing the member view and the admin view for each area, questions included.

- One paragraph: what is finished, what is not, and what AI does in your solution.

Not needed: slides, a pitch, a business case.

## Rules

- Teams of 1 or 2. Coding with AI is expected.

- Code written before Saturday 9:30 only if it is public and generic.

- You keep your code. By submitting you give us a permanent right to use and modify it. An open-source licence is recommended.

- The judging criteria and weights below do not change after Saturday 9:30.

## How we judge

We judge what the platform does for us, not how it is built. Four criteria, each scored 1 to 10 by the mentor and the organizers:

- Usefulness, 40%. Would we use it next week as it is?

- Completeness, 25%. Do all three areas work, for members and admins, on the sandbox data?

- Quality, 20%. Is it fast, clear and pleasant for someone who opens it twice a week?

- Rebuildability, 15%. Can we redeploy it from your repository and your one-page description without you? This is the only place we look at the technical side.
